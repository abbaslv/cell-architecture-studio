Plant cell

A photosynthetic plant cell: cellulose wall, large central vacuole, and chloroplasts pressed against the periphery.

This schematic shows a mesophyll cell, the photosynthetic tissue inside a leaf. Unlike an animal cell, it is enclosed by a cellulose cell wall and usually dominated by a large central vacuole. Chloroplasts sit near the plasma membrane, where light and CO₂ are available. The model is illustrative: it does not represent cell size, organelle counts, or membrane ultrastructure to scale.

Species: Elodea sp. · Aquatic plant in the family Hydrocharitaceae. Elodea leaf cells are a classic school microscopy subject because their chloroplasts are easy to observe. Scale: All geometry is schematic and unitless. No real-world scale is inferred from the model; the size note is a separate textbook range.

White blood cell

A motile immune cell with a multi-lobed nucleus, enzyme-rich granules, and actin-driven pseudopods.

Neutrophils patrol blood and tissue, following chemical cues to sites of infection. They are specialised for rapid response: a lobed nucleus, abundant granules, and a flexible actin cytoskeleton let them squeeze through tissue and engulf microbes. This schematic emphasises those features; granule counts and nuclear lobe shape vary between real cells.

Species: Homo sapiens · Human neutrophil, the most abundant circulating leukocyte and a first responder to bacterial infection. Scale: Schematic and unitless. The pseudopods and granules are enlarged for legibility; no real-world scale is encoded.

Neuron

A signal-conducting cell with a receptive soma and dendrites, a long axon, insulating myelin, and transmitter-releasing terminals.

Neurons are polarised cells built for communication. Dendrites collect inputs, the soma integrates them, and the axon carries an action potential to terminals that release neurotransmitter onto the next cell. The myelin sheath shown here is a schematic of the insulating wrapping provided by glial cells. Real neurons vary enormously in shape, and many have thousands of synaptic contacts not depicted here.

Species: Mammalian (schematic) · A generic mammalian multipolar neuron. Axon length and myelination vary widely between neuron types and species. Scale: Schematic and unitless. Axon length is compressed; the model cannot be used to estimate real dimensions.

Epithelial cell

A polarised absorptive cell: a dense apical brush border, a sealing tight junction, and energy-hungry basolateral transport.

Enterocytes line the small intestine and absorb nutrients. Their apical surface carries a dense brush border of microvilli, while their basolateral surface contacts neighbouring cells and the basement membrane. Tight junctions separate these domains and limit leakage between cells. This schematic highlights that polarity rather than the full cytoskeletal and membrane architecture.

Species: Homo sapiens · A columnar absorptive cell from the small-intestinal lining. Enterocytes are polarised: their apical and basolateral surfaces face different environments. Scale: Schematic and unitless. Microvillus density and membrane thickness are exaggerated for legibility.

Along the midline sit the corpus callosum, thalamus, hypothalamus, pituitary gland, ventricular cavities, brainstem, and cerebellum, framed by the C-shaped limbic system—cingulate gyrus, fornix, hippocampus, and amygdala—that links emotion, motivation, and long-term memory. Toggle Cutaway to hide the remaining left cortical hemisphere and view the subcortical organs in isolation.

Species: Homo sapiens · Open sagittal and subcortical cutout of the adult human brain. Deep paired structures are shown in full 3D relief against the recessed left hemisphere so each limbic and diencephalic part can be selected directly. Scale: Schematic and unitless. Deep nuclei, ventricles, and cranial nerves are spaced slightly for visual separation and interactive picking rather than surgical precision.

Interactive camera, annotation & accessibility controls

  • Orbit & zoom (OrbitControls): Drag to orbit with damped smoothing (pan disabled; polar angle clamped between 0.12π and 0.88π; zoom distance 1.05 to 8.5).
  • Mesh vs. Focus view modes: Mesh frames the whole specimen; Focus smoothly animates CameraDirector onto the selected structure's 3D bounding box.
  • Cutaway: Toggles visibility of outer shell parts marked cutawayHide (e.g., cell walls, membranes, cortical hemispheres) to inspect internal organelles and subcortical anatomy.
  • Auto-rotate & Reset view: Auto-rotate slowly orbits the specimen (disabled automatically when prefers-reduced-motion is active, and pauses while dragging or when the browser tab is hidden); Reset view restores the canonical framing.
  • Save PNG: Captures the WebGL stage (preserveDrawingBuffer: true) and downloads a PNG snapshot.
  • Annotation & picking sync: Clicking a 3D mesh (PointerPicker raycasting) or activating a button in the keyboard-operable Structures list selects the same structure, updates the detail inspector (summary, function, schematic mesh-to-structure mapping, biology review status, schematic notes, cited draft facts, and a local-only copyable Report a correction template), and lets learners save favourites.
  • Accessibility & theme: Skip link to main content, dark/light/system color theme toggle, reduced-motion support, tested at 320 px viewport width, and automatic static SVG + full-text fallback whenever WebGL is unavailable.

3D rendering & architecture details

  • Imperative Three.js engine (@cas/viewer-core): Uses WebGLRenderer (antialias, alpha, preserveDrawingBuffer: true, SRGBColorSpace, ACESFilmicToneMapping with exposure 1.05, device pixel ratio capped at 2) and a PerspectiveCamera with 38° vertical FOV.
  • Studio lighting & stage: Image-based lighting generated via PMREMGenerator and RoomEnvironment, combined with a 4-light studio rig (hemisphere fill, warm directional key light, cool rim light, and soft under-fill) over a translucent circular pedestal ring and disc.
  • Procedural schematic geometry: All 12 specimens (1,041 instances, ~329,534 estimated triangles) are constructed deterministically from 27 repository-authored procedural primitives (including organic blobs, biconcave discs, curved sheets, helical tubes, tapered branches, and brain lobe/tract geometries) and seeded repeat patterns (scatter, shell, ring, line, helix, grid).   • Lazy chunk isolation: Three.js and procedural geometry generators load in a separate async viewer chunk so the initial shell stays lightweight; deterministic SVG previews are rendered from the same expanded scene instances.
