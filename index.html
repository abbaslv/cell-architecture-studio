Plant cell

A photosynthetic plant cell: cellulose wall, large central vacuole, and chloroplasts pressed against the periphery.

This schematic shows a mesophyll cell, the photosynthetic tissue inside a leaf. Unlike an animal cell, it is enclosed by a cellulose cell wall and usually dominated by a large central vacuole. Chloroplasts sit near the plasma membrane, where light and CO₂ are available. The model is illustrative: it does not represent cell size, organelle counts, or membrane ultrastructure to scale.

Species: Elodea sp. · Aquatic plant in the family Hydrocharitaceae. Elodea leaf cells are a classic school microscopy subject because their chloroplasts are easy to observe. Scale: All geometry is schematic and unitless. No real-world scale is inferred from the model; the size note is a separate textbook range.

White blood cell

A motile immune cell with a multi-lobed nucleus, enzyme-rich granules, and actin-driven pseudopods.

Neutrophils patrol blood and tissue, following chemical cues to sites of infection. They are specialised for rapid response: a lobed nucleus, abundant granules, and a flexible actin cytoskeleton let them squeeze through tissue and engulf microbes. This schematic emphasises those features; granule counts and nuclear lobe shape vary between real cells.

Species: Homo sapiens · Human neutrophil, the most abundant circulating leukocyte and a first responder to bacterial infection. Scale: Schematic and unitless. The pseudopods and granules are enlarged for legibility; no real-world scale is encoded.

Neuron

A signal-conducting cell with a receptive soma and dendrites, a long axon, insulating myelin, and transmitter-releasing terminals.

Neurons are polarised cells built for communication. Dendrites collect inputs, the soma integrates them, and the axon carries an action potential to terminals that release neurotransmitter onto the next cell. The myelin sheath shown here is a schematic of the insulating wrapping provided by glial cells. Real neurons vary enormously in shape, and many have thousands of synaptic contacts not depicted here.

Species: Mammalian (schematic) · A generic mammalian multipolar neuron. Axon length and myelination vary widely between neuron types and species. Scale: Schematic and unitless. Axon length is compressed; the model cannot be used to estimate real dimensions.

Epithelial cell

A polarised absorptive cell: a dense apical brush border, a sealing tight junction, and energy-hungry basolateral transport.

Enterocytes line the small intestine and absorb nutrients. Their apical surface carries a dense brush border of microvilli, while their basolateral surface contacts neighbouring cells and the basement membrane. Species: Homo sapiens · A columnar absorptive cell from the small-intestinal lining. Enterocytes are polarised: their apical and basolateral surfaces face different environments. Scale: Schematic and unitless. Microvillus density and membrane thickness are exaggerated for legibility.
